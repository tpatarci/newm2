## The arm floor: four per arm, and why it is not six

**Four is the DERIVED minimum**, and the arithmetic is stated so a reader can
check it rather than take it. Under perfect separation on balanced arms of size
`n` the exact one-sided test returns `1 / C(2n, n)`:

- three per arm: `1 / C(6,3) = 1/20 = 0.050` — does **not** clear `p < 0.05`
- four per arm: `1 / C(8,4) = 1/70 ≈ 0.0143` — clears it

Four is therefore the smallest balanced table that can reach the threshold at
all.

**An earlier draft of this plan set the floor at six, and the correction is
recorded rather than silently dropped**, because the record's whole standard is
that its figures are checkable. Six was a planner's choice two steps above the
reachable minimum, justified by the claim that six *was* that minimum. That claim
was arithmetically false, and the consequence was not cosmetic: five per arm with
perfect separation gives `1 / C(10,5) ≈ 0.004`, clearing the pre-registered test
by roughly a factor of twelve, and **a floor of six would have routed that
clearing table to `NOT-ATTRIBUTED`** — ending the attribution effort under a
terminal rule on the strength of a reading that had already answered the
question.

**What lowering it costs, stated rather than left implicit:** at exactly four per
arm the test admits only perfect separation (3 of 4 against 0 of 4 gives
`5/70 ≈ 0.071` and fails), so an `ATTRIBUTED` ruling could rest on as few as
eight paired observations. **This measurement does not rest on the floor** — it
rests on 40 paired observations in the criterion-conformant table and 129 in the
wider one.

one-sided comparison against 0 red of 10 gives `p ≈ 0.095`, clearing `p < 0.05`
needs 0 red of 16, and a 0.40 → 0.20 improvement needs roughly 36 runs for 80%
