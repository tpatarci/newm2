Two of those eleven deserve a note, since a reader could mistake either for a defect:

- **`rule-match-`** is a fragment, not a key — it is the `rule-match-*` glob as it appears in prose on both sides, and `grep -oE 'rule-[a-z-]+'` cannot tell a glob from a key. It matches on both sides and therefore cancels.
- **`rule-match-name`** is the removed spelling. It appears in the notes only in the migration note at L217, which tells an existing config what to change, and in `src/Config.cpp` only in an explanatory comment. The plan predicted exactly this — "the audit as written today produces no difference at all, because `src/Config.cpp` still carries the removed spelling in an explanatory comment" — and that prediction held. **The one intentional asymmetry the plan names is therefore invisible to this particular audit rather than reported by it.** That is a real limit of the command, not of the record: an audit that only compares key *sets* cannot distinguish "documented as working" from "documented as removed". The distinction was confirmed by reading L217-221, which is intact.

No key is present on one side only. Nothing needed correcting, so no line of `docs/RELEASE-NOTES.md:191-228` was edited by this plan.

## Where the shipped notes and the checklist are deliberately worded differently

The plan's `<output>` asked for this explicitly. `COMPILED_CODE_BEHAVIOR_CHECKLIST.md` is a signoff record for developers; `docs/RELEASE-NOTES.md` is read by a user deciding whether to trust a target. Three places diverge on purpose:

1. **The retired TightVNC rationale is quoted in the checklist and only named in the notes.** The checklist keeps D-8-TIGHTVNC's original sentence verbatim, because *why* it was wrong is the transferable part of the record. The shipped notes say only that an earlier draft skipped TightVNC on an argument from the two servers being related, that the argument failed on the two extensions it named, and that the general form is worth recognising — "a family resemblance between two servers is not a measurement of either one." A user does not need the retired paragraph; reproducing it in a shipped document risks it being read as a live claim, which the plan's prohibition forbids.
2. **The checklist names the owner and the follow-up; the notes do not.** "Whoever next stands up a remote-desktop validation session" is a project-internal commitment. The notes carry the user-facing half instead: what is unexercised, and that a bug found there is worth reporting.
3. **The checklist cites `x2goserver:amd64`; the notes cite `x2goserver-common:all 4.1.0.3-5`.** Both stamps are `2026-08-29 17:43` in `/var/log/dpkg.log`. The notes quote the `-common` line because that is the one the verification report cited when it disproved the "never installed" claim independently, so a reader re-running the check lands on the same line the auditor did.
